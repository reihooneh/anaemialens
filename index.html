    How it works

    1. Capture

      The user pulls down the lower eyelid and takes a photo with any phone camera.

    2. Check the photo

      The app refuses to guess on a dark, blurry or glare-covered photo and asks for a retake instead.

    3. Correct the light

      The white of the eye should be neutral. Any tint it shows comes from the room light, so the app removes that tint from the eyelid too.

    4. Measure pallor

      It measures how red the eyelid tissue is. Low redness means pallor, a classic clinical sign of anaemia, and the app advises a blood test.

    Working in this demo

    • Photo upload and marking
    • Brightness, sharpness and glare checks
    • Lighting correction from the white of the eye
    • Redness measurement, raw and corrected

    Comes next in the full project

    • Thresholds here are hand-set for the demo and are not medically calibrated
    • Replace them with a model trained on the CP-AnemiC dataset (710 children, lab-measured haemoglobin)
    • Test it on adults from Italy and India (Eyes-defy-anemia) to see if it generalises
    • Automatic eyelid detection, so no manual marking
